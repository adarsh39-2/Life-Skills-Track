# Listening and Active Communication

### Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

Active listening means giving full attention to the speaker and understanding what they are trying to communicate.

Some strategies for active listening are:

- **Avoid distractions:** Focus on the speaker instead of checking your phone or thinking about other things.
- **Don't interrupt:** Let the speaker finish before responding.
- **Show interest:** Use phrases like "I see" or "Tell me more" to show that you are listening.
- **Use body language:** Maintain eye contact and nod to show attention.
- **Ask questions:** Ask whenever something is not clear.
- **Take notes:** Write down important points during meetings or discussions.
- **Paraphrase:** Repeat important points in your own words to confirm your understanding.

### Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

Reflective listening means understanding the speaker's message and feelings and checking whether we have understood them correctly.

The key points are:

- Listen carefully and focus on what the speaker is saying.
- Repeat the message in your own words.
- Ask the speaker if you understood correctly.
- Avoid judging, arguing or giving advice immediately.
- Allow the speaker to clarify if something is misunderstood.
- Repeat the process until both sides understand each other.
- Pay attention to emotions and body language, not just words.

### Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

Some obstacles that affect my listening are:

- Getting distracted by my phone or notifications.
- Thinking about my reply while someone is speaking.
- Making assumptions before the speaker finishes.
- Losing concentration during long conversations.
- Being tired or stressed.
- Background noise and other disturbances.
- Assuming that I already know something and not paying proper attention.

### Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

I can improve my listening by making a few changes in my daily habits:

- Keep my phone aside during conversations.
- Focus on the speaker instead of preparing my reply.
- Let others finish before responding.
- Take notes during important meetings and technical discussions.
- Ask questions instead of making assumptions.
- Repeat important points to confirm my understanding.
- Maintain eye contact and avoid distractions.
- Get enough sleep to improve concentration.

### Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I sometimes use passive communication when I feel uncomfortable expressing my opinions.

- When talking to teachers, seniors or interviewers, I hesitate because I don't want to say something wrong.
- When I want to avoid arguments, I prefer to stay quiet.
- When everyone in a group disagrees with me, I sometimes avoid sharing my opinion.
- When I am unsure about something, I hesitate to ask questions.
- Sometimes I say "It's fine" even when I am not happy with the situation.

### Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

I may become aggressive when I am angry, frustrated or under pressure.

- When someone repeatedly interrupts me.
- When someone ignores my suggestions.
- When I feel I am being blamed unfairly.
- When I am already stressed and something goes wrong.
- When I keep my frustration inside for too long.

In such situations, I may raise my voice, interrupt others or respond rudely.

### Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

Sometimes I don't express my anger or disappointment directly. Instead, it comes out through my behaviour.

- **Sarcasm:** Making sarcastic comments when I am annoyed with someone.
- **Silent treatment:** Avoiding someone instead of explaining why I am upset.
- **Taunts:** Making indirect comments like "Nice of you to finally show up."
- **Gossiping:** Sharing my frustration with others instead of discussing the problem directly.
- **Indirect behaviour:** Showing my disagreement through my actions instead of communicating openly.

This usually happens when I want to avoid confrontation but still want the other person to know that I am unhappy.

### Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive? What steps can you apply in your own life?

Assertive communication means expressing my thoughts and feelings clearly while respecting other people's opinions.

I can improve my communication by following these steps:

- **Use "I" statements:** Explain how I feel instead of blaming others.
- **Be clear and direct:** Express my opinions without hesitation.
- **Stay calm:** Take a moment before responding when I am angry.
- **Use confident body language:** Maintain eye contact and speak in a normal tone.
- **Learn to say no:** Politely refuse when I already have too much work.
- **Respect others' opinions:** Listen to different viewpoints even when I disagree.
- **Speak up at the right time:** Discuss problems instead of keeping frustration inside.
- **Focus on the problem:** Discuss the issue without personally attacking anyone.
- **Be ready to compromise:** Try to find a solution that works for everyone.
