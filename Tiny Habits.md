### Question 2: How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P?

B = MAP means Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt. According to BJ Fogg, these three things are needed to perform any behavior.

- **M – Motivation:** It means how much we want to do something. For example, I want to improve my coding skills.
- **A – Ability:** It means making the task easy to do. Instead of studying coding for two hours, I can start by solving just one question daily.
- **P – Prompt:** It is a reminder or trigger that tells us when to perform the habit. For example, after having breakfast, I will solve one coding question.

By using this formula, I can start new habits without putting too much pressure on myself.

### Question 3: Why is it important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?

Shine means feeling happy and proud after completing a small habit. It is important because it makes us feel good about our progress and encourages us to repeat the same action.

For example, if I complete one coding question, I can appreciate myself by saying, "Good job!" Even if the task is very small, celebrating it gives a positive feeling. I learned that we should celebrate small achievements instead of always focusing on big results. This helps us build confidence and make habits stronger.

### Question 5: What is the book's perspective about Identity?

According to the book *Atomic Habits*, building a habit is not just about changing what we do but also about changing how we see ourselves.

For example, instead of saying "I want to learn coding," I should start thinking "I am a person who codes every day." When we connect our habits with our identity, we become more serious about following them.

The book explains that every small action is like a vote for the type of person we want to become. So, we should focus on becoming a better person rather than only focusing on goals.

### Question 6: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?

According to *Atomic Habits*, we should make good habits as easy as possible. We can do this by following a few simple steps:

1. Keep everything needed for the habit ready in advance.
2. Start with small actions instead of difficult tasks.
3. Connect a new habit with an existing daily routine.
4. Reduce unnecessary steps that make the habit difficult.

For example, if I want to read books daily, I can keep a book near my bed so that I can easily read a few pages before sleeping.

The main idea is to reduce the effort required to start a habit.

### Question 7: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?

The book suggests making bad habits difficult by adding more steps between ourselves and the unwanted behavior.

For example:

1. Keep your phone away while studying.
2. Turn off unnecessary notifications.
3. Remove distracting apps from your home screen.
4. Log out of social media accounts so that opening them takes extra effort.

If we make bad habits less convenient, we are less likely to repeat them. The main idea is to create an environment where good habits are easy and bad habits are difficult.

### Question 8: Pick one habit that you would like to do more of? What are the steps that you can take to make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and/or the response satisfying?

One habit I would like to develop is solving DSA questions every day. Sometimes I skip practice because I feel lazy or think that I need a lot of time to solve questions.

To make this habit easier, I will take the following steps:

- **Make the cue obvious:** I will keep my laptop ready and set a daily reminder for coding practice.
- **Make it attractive:** I will choose interesting problems and track my daily progress.
- **Make it easy:** I will start by solving just one question daily instead of setting a big target.
- **Make it satisfying:** After solving a question, I will mark it as completed and appreciate my progress.

By following these steps, I hope to make coding practice a regular part of my daily routine.

### Question 9: Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of? What are the steps that you can take to make the cue invisible or the process unattractive or hard or the response unsatisfying?

One habit I would like to reduce is spending too much time scrolling through Instagram and other social media apps. Sometimes I open these apps just to check something, but end up wasting a lot of time.

To reduce this habit, I will take the following steps:

- **Make the cue invisible:** I will remove social media apps from my phone's home screen and turn off their notifications.
- **Make it unattractive:** I will remind myself that excessive scrolling wastes my time and affects my productivity.
- **Make it difficult:** I will set app time limits and keep my phone away while studying.
- **Make it unsatisfying:** I will track the time I waste on these apps and try to reduce it every week.

By following these steps, I can reduce unnecessary screen time and use that time for more useful activities.
